<h1>Preamble and Synopsis</h1><p>Passkey's flagship event management system - <em>Manage</em>, or colloquially, <em>Resdesk</em> - is a large legacy monolith application that is falling behind in the realm of security updates.  This is largely due to it's deeply-rooted entanglement with a few very old, very inactive libraries for which support and maintenance has been abandoned in favour of newer architectures.</p><p>At the top of this list of abandonware is Apache Struts v1.  The original release of Struts dates back to the spring of 2000 and it's final maintenance release (1.3.10) was delivered a little better than eight years later in the fall of 2008.  The Resdesk application was undergoing new development at that time and incorporated the original Struts framework into its architecture.  As time passed, Struts was replaced with its next major iteration - <em>Struts 2</em> - and new application development in Resdesk shifted accordingly.  Several existing Resdesk components originally written with Struts v1 were migrated to Struts v2, but as the sheer size and scope of this effort materialized as unmanageable, additional investments into the effort were postponed indefinitely.</p><p>Sadly, Resdesk continues to cling to the Struts v1 library, along with its equally outdated dependencies, in order to avoid a very significant overhaul effort that would likely be measured in <em>months</em>.  It has been difficult to create a business justification for this kind of expense and imposed delay to feature production.</p><h1>Purpose</h1><p>This investigation proposes an alternate sort of investment that has the potential to solve this dependency problem in less time and effort than it would take a dev team to do it.</p><p>Over the last year or two, Cvent has campaigned to sharply ramp-up developer use of AI-driven tooling in order to help developers overcome common time sinks and mundane automatable tasks.  More recently, these tools have improved to be increasingly capable of handling tasks of greater complexity, and a new campaign to identify innovative and creative ways to put that ability to use in our business has sprouted.</p><p>The purpose of this investigation is to identify whether any of the AI tools available to Cvent are able to meaningfully contribute to the abandoned Struts 1 upgrade effort.  A positive report returned by this investigation could possibly reignite the initiative and put long-term Resdesk objectives like containerization back within reach.</p><p><br Limitations</h3><p>Three days were allotted to this investigation. At the best of times, this would not be enough for me to spend the time I want to spend investigating the best use of these tools for this purpose.  Unfortunately, due to an unpredictably busy sprint, I was only able to use about a third of that allotment across the entire two week sprint.</p><h3>Tool Limitations</h3><p><strong>Marketing vs Reality</strong>: Obtaining starting momentum took some time, as trial-and-error led the way in determining the best combinations of tools to use.  Early attempts to use the tools that had the <em>appearance</em> of best fit and convenience for Passkey actually ended with frustration.  Also, as migration prompts are computation-intensive, commands often took a long while to fail over - sometimes over an hour before you are able to identify that you're not going to get a valid result.</p><p><strong>Filesets</strong>: I discovered that basic plugin modes ('Ask' and 'Edit') work only with small filesets.  In IntelliJ, for instance, Copilot will only let me work with 10 files at a time. Amazon Q, on the other hand, would not work with Resdesk because it was too big.</p><p><strong>Rate Limiting</strong>: I hit Copilot request rate limits even in my most successful prompts.  While this did not fully reset my session context with the LLM, it broke the workflow and disoriented Copilot enough that I could not merely tell it to 'continue'.  I had to show it what it started doing before it was interrupted, and what left had to be done.</p><h3>Filters and Censored Responses</h3><p>A known limitation to many Edit prompts is the dreaded 'public code' filter.  We have no control over this filter, and it is intended to mitigate the potential legal concerns regarding the use of publicly available code that may be differently licensed then the way we intend to use it.</p><p>In addition to its phobia of public code, Copilot seemed to be very timid of providing solutions to problems involving legacy libraries.  While I was able to ask general questions about Struts in Ask mode, it was difficult to get it to be contextually conversational about Struts in edit mode.  Whenever I asked it to change a struts-related file, it would produce an answer and then immediately censor it.  My theory is that Copilot is 'taking the fifth' - protecting itself from litigation by not responding to questions involving technologies that are implicitly high risk and vulnerable.  It probably wants to make sure that whatever it recommends for me to do, it is a safe and secure practice.  I was eventually able to coerce Copilot around this issue once I figured out why it was probably making these decisions.</p><p>This was a fun conversation:</p><p>Me: <em>&quot;Give me a list of Struts v1 Action classes in the web/src/main/java/com/passkey/resdesk/actions directory and all its sub-directories&quot;</em></p><p>Copilot: &quot;<em>Sure!  </em>*CENSORED*<em>&quot;</em></p><p>Me: <em>&quot;Uh, why did your last response get censored?&quot;</em></p><p>Copilot: <em>&quot;It didn't&quot;</em></p><p>Me: <em>...&quot;I'm staring the word 'censored' across my screen right now.  Why are you hallucinating that the answer didn't get censored?&quot;</em></p><p>Copilot: <em>&quot;I'm not.&quot;</em></p><p><br /></p><p><br /></p><p>First Investigation: IntelliJ GitHub Copilot (w/ GPT and Anthropic)</p><ul style="list-style-type: square;"><li data-uuid="6b457a49-56a0-45fe-80c1-9c00716aac9d">Outcome: Frustration.<ul style="list-style-type: square;"><li data-uuid="96967710-8255-41fe-b3a0-7bc5af2a9ac7">GPT is low-feedback and does not pontificate openly when it strategizes a solution for your question</li><li data-uuid="c7fcf45d-4c6a-4bf5-a036-62f672a52cbc">Many encounters with censored results as I tried to get Struts 1 classes up to parity with the Struts 2.</li><li data-uuid="e129f7fd-f800-4e3d-8ee5-1b88f9486de9">Unintuitive fileset context in Edit mode: I had to manually select files to include in its 'Edit mode' context, which was clunky.</li><li data-uuid="d16a67e5-8865-4a1c-b36f-a2f5ee40560d">No Agent Mode in Copilot for IntelliJ (I understand this is coming soon). Estimates</h1><h3>Would I recommend more investment in trying to use AI for the Struts upgrade?</h3><p>Yes I would.  For now, I would recommend using the <strong>GitHub Copilot plugin for VSCode</strong> in <em>Agent</em> mode, and manually stopping execution when things get out of control.  I would also recommend targeting the upgrade to the latest Struts version available - <em>not</em>  the currently-used version of Struts2 in resdesk - in order to avoid censored/filtered responses, which plagued me for the first three hours of this investigation.</p><p>Further, I would
