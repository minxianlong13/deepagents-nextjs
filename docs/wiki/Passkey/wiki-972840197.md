## Summary

In the PBB payment wallet, updating billing information (cardholder name, address, postal code, or phone) clears the stored credit card data and expiration date on a reservation.  Users cannot fix billing details without re-entering the full card.   
Although the upgrade to PBB payment wallet provided us a lot more security benefits in the platform, there was a negative effect to the user experience, making modifying reservations inefficient. 

This restriction blocked the ability to make bulk updates in RLM (Passkey tool for processing room lists) if the RLM uploader didn't know the full CC details when making a change.  Ex - If there are invalid or missing billing fields in a dropped off rooming list, then they get completely blocked from being able to make the credit card association valid because they no longer have access to re-enter all the card details from the original upload in order to correct missing or incorrect billing info.

Multiple customers reported the issues they were facing last year after the upgrade, and RLM was prioritized at the time to fix.  

**Where we are today:** RLM supports billing-only fixes, single and bulk, through a workaround we built in Passkey (Option B discussed later). The other channels do not support billing-only updates yet. In those channels a user must enter a whole new card and billing to change anything.

**Preferred direction: That the payment wallet team supports billing-only updates natively, so every channel shares one UX and one source of truth (see Option A below).**

## Problem And Goals

The PBB wallet treats card and billing as one unit. Saving billing changes without the card details wipes out the card.

**Problem summary**

-   Updating billing in the wallet (for example, correcting an address or postal code) clears other fields, including stored credit card data.
-   This is especially painful in bulk changes, where users must re-enter card details for every reservation, which adds manual work and risk of error.
-   It was not treated as a bug, but as a limitation of the wallet's current design.
-   Internal teams and customers want to update billing independently of card fields.
-   PYMNT-2180 notes that billing-only updates exist today **only through the PBB APIs, not in the wallet form**.

**Goals**

1.  Let users in every reservation channel in Passkey change billing only, card only, or both.
2.  Keep one version of the payment UX and one source of truth, ideally owned by the wallet.

### Current state in RLM

RLM already uses Option B. We split the card and the billing:

-   The payment wallet handles the card details, and the wallet's own billing fields are hidden.
-   Our billing form sits directly under the card.
-   Billing updates are saved through the APIs.
-   This supports fixing billing for a single reservation and in bulk, without re-entering the card.

It works, but it is not the ideal end state. It had to be built out, and none of the other channels have it.

## GroupLink Inbound Findings

We reviewed how inbound modifications from hotel systems (GroupLink) treat the card and billing information. In short, GroupLink is **likely not affected by the wallet limitation, but this is unconfirmed.**

-   **Same card:** Mapping rule 360 (LMS, vendor 450) preserves the stored card number when the card type and last 4 digits match the inbound token.
-   **New card:** Under the same rule, if the card type or last 4 digits differ, the inbound token is accepted and stored.
-   **Not the wallet:** Inbound cards are vendor tokens. For LMS they are stored as a custom payment (RESCUSTOMPAYMENT and EXTRESERVATIONDATA), not through the PBB wallet.
-   **Billing address gap:** The inbound payment card carries no billing address for HMS or LMS. LMS fills it from the guest profile address, and HMS likely needs the same. Mapping rule 1100 (IHG) blocks guest address updates on inbound modifications, which is a guest address rule rather than a billing rule.
-   **Not documented:** The field-level modify logic for an inbound reservation is marked "to be reviewed later" in the inbound documentation. The investigation notes also appear to predate the wallet.

A GroupLink developer should confirm this, or it should be tested in a lower environment, before we call it done.

Sources: [Inbound Reservation and Status transfers](https://wiki.cvent.com/pages/viewpage.action?pageId=483309179), [Cvent Passkey GroupLink Mapping Rule Definitions](https://wiki.cvent.com/pages/viewpage.action?pageId=65656412), [Dev notes (GROUP-89760)](https://wiki.cvent.com/pages/viewpage.action?pageId=533002227).

## Appendix: RLM Workflow Showing The Problem

This is how the problem showed up in RLM, the first use case, before the fix described under Current State in RLM.

Details...

  

An organizer or hotel uploads a roomlist. The billing information may be partial or incorrect, but we can assume the card and number are valid. In this example, some billing information is blank.

They map the roomlist with the Smart Mapping Assistant or manually, and then proceed. If the organizer or hotel checks the preview fields, they will already see that some data is missing.

The reservations are submitted and may or may not have errors. In this example, some reservations are missing billing information. This is indicated by the "!" and by pink fields with errors.

The user now wants to fix the address fields in bulk so the reservations can be submitted. The initial state is "no changes to billing", but they can switch this to credit card.

When they open the editor, they cannot enter just some billing information and propagate it to the selected reservations. They must enter the credit card number, expiration and billing name, which may vary per reservation, and they do not know what to use. They only wanted to update billing information.

**So they could not bulk edit the reservations unless they provided complete details, saved, and then applied them to all the reservations.**

In the same way, if they edited reservations individually, they needed to enter the card again and could not fix only the billing information.
